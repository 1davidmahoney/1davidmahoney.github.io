Direct Printing / Short Print Run

We pay a printer to manufacture a batch of books. The books are delivered to us, and we control what happens to them.

Possible distribution

  • Personally distribute copies to museums
  • Approach local bookstores
  • Sell wholesale to bookstores
  • Offer books on consignment
  • Sell at events, talks, or signings
  • Sell directly and ship copies ourselves
  • Give copies away where appropriate
Work we'd need to plan:
  • Editing and proofreading
  • Interior book layout
  • Cover design
  • Choose book size, binding, paper, color, etc.
  • Get printer quotes
  • Order and review a proof
  • Decide how many copies to print
  • Store the books
  • Contact museums and bookstores
  • Handle inventory and sales if applicable

Print on Demand

Instead of buying a large inventory, we upload the finished book to a print-on-demand service.

When someone orders the book, the service prints a copy and ships it to the customer.

Work we'd need to plan:
  • Edit and proofread the manuscript
  • Create print-ready interior files
  • Create the cover
  • Decide ISBN/publishing arrangements
  • Choose price
  • Upload book and description
  • Order and review a proof
  • Choose distribution channels

Direct Distribution + Online Availability

This combines two approaches.

Local/direct: We obtain copies ourselves and personally distribute them to museums, bookstores, events, and other organizations.

Online: A print-on-demand service lets anyone else order the book without us having to pack and ship every copy.

Our local copies could either be author copies purchased through the print-on-demand service or a separate batch purchased from a commercial printer.

Important comparison:
  • Cost of POD author copies
  • Cost per book from a separate printer
  • Minimum quantities
  • Print quality
  • Shipping costs
  • Storage needs

Traditional Publishing

We try to persuade an existing publisher to acquire and publish the book.

Possible targets could include:

  • Commercial publishers
  • Regional publishers
  • Local-history publishers
  • Specialty publishers
  • University or academic presses, if appropriate
Likely work:
  • Finish/polish the manuscript
  • Research appropriate publishers
  • Determine whether an agent is required
  • Prepare queries or a book proposal
  • Submit sample chapters/manuscript
  • Wait for responses
  • Possibly revise and resubmit

Who will do the production work?

For each part of the book, we can do it ourselves, hire a freelancer, or pay a publishing/printing service.

  • Editing
  • Proofreading
  • Fact checking
  • Permissions/citations
  • Interior design and typesetting
  • Photo/illustration preparation
  • Cover design
  • ISBN and barcode
  • Copyright/front/back matter
  • Print-file preparation
  • Proof review
  • Pricing
  • Distribution setup
  • Marketing
  • Museum/bookstore outreach

Ebook Only

Prepare the book as a digital publication rather than manufacturing printed copies.

Likely work:
  • Edit and proofread manuscript
  • Format ebook
  • Create ebook cover
  • Prepare title/description/metadata
  • Choose sales platforms
  • Choose price
  • Upload and test

Audiobook

Major decisions:

  • Dad narrates it himself?
  • Hire a narrator?
  • Record/edit it ourselves?
  • Hire an audiobook producer?
  • Which audiobook stores/services should carry it?

An audiobook can also be added later after the printed and/or ebook editions are finished.

Possible Plan: Local / Direct Publishing

Basic model:

Finish the book → prepare print-ready files → hire a printer → purchase a batch → personally distribute books to museums, local bookstores, events, and other interested groups.

Possible Plan: Print on Demand

Finish and design the book → upload it to a print-on-demand service → make it available for ordering → service prints and ships copies as needed.

Possible Plan: Hybrid Self-Publishing

Prepare one professionally finished book.

Obtain physical copies for personal distribution to museums, bookstores, events, and other local outlets.

At the same time, make the book available through print-on-demand so people elsewhere can order it online.

Optionally add an ebook and audiobook.

Possible Plan: Traditional Publishing

Polish the manuscript → identify appropriate publishers and/or agents → prepare submission materials → submit the book → pursue publication offers.

If that route doesn't work or proves undesirable, self-publishing remains available later.
